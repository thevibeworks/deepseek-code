Conditions: dsc M1 (src/ provider+loop+4 tools, headless -p), /anthropic

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| explore-answer | 3 | 3/3 | 14.7 | 6 | 18029 | 82 | 0.0008 |
| feature-tdd | 3 | 3/3 | 17.2 | 4 | 13901 | 85 | 0.0011 |

Milestone 1 exit gate: PASSED — 9/9 success at the token and cost
figures above. The gate was set against a mainstream agent CLI
retargeted to DeepSeek, measured privately on the same tasks and
fixtures; dsc came in roughly an order of magnitude lower on
tokens/solve and several times lower on $/solve. That comparison arm
is not part of this repo (the harness is proprietary), so those rows
are omitted here and the numbers above stand on their own.

The mechanism behind the gap is prefix size: dsc's stable prefix is
~1.1k tokens, where full-featured harnesses commonly carry 15k+.

Caveat recorded: an earlier same-day dsc matrix (runs.jsonl rows 11-19)
went 7/9 — bugfix-slugify failed 2/3 because the system prompt's
"minimal, surgical changes" guideline steered flash into appending
compensating replaces instead of fixing the buggy regex. Fixed by the
root-cause guideline in src/engine/prompt.ts. Prompt wording is
behavior; eval-gate every prompt change.

Caveat updated 2026-08-05: the M1 3/3 on bugfix-slugify was
optimistic. Under byte-identical prompt bytes, 15 further runs went
10/15 — the band-aid pattern (keep `[^a-z0-9]`, append collapse+trim
replaces) recurs at roughly 1-in-3 by sampling alone. Strengthening
the guideline ("If a regex or condition is wrong, fix that regex or
condition rather than patching its output afterwards", gated
2026-08-05) moved it to 5/6 with no regression on the other tasks
(4/4). Treat dsc/flash slugify pass rate as ~80-85%, not 100%; judge
gates on n>=6.

## parallel-explore — pinned 2026-08-05 (M4 exit gate: NOT MET)

Six independent config-layering questions across six services in one
monorepo fixture (77 KB, ~12 files/service; answer chains hand-written,
bulk generated by gen-bulk.ts which asserts the bulk never contains
answer vocabulary). DSC_CONTEXT_BUDGET=24000. The task prompt tells the
agent to use parallel sub-agents if available, so the arms differ only
in whether the task tool exists — this measures the MECHANISM, not
flash's spontaneous delegation policy (see caveats).

| adapter | n | success | wall s (med) | turns | tokens/solve (med) | cache% | $/solve (med) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dsc-solo (= today's default dsc) | 4 | 4/4 | 22.3 | 8.5 | 47887 | 82 | 0.0020 |
| dsc-fanout (6 sub-agents) | 4 | 4/4 | 55.5 | 8.5 | 248158 | 77 | 0.0133 |

Gate was "parallel exploration beats single-agent wall-clock at
comparable cost". Result: fan-out is 2.49x SLOWER and 6.75x more
EXPENSIVE at identical success. Recorded as failed rather than retuned.

Consequence: sub-agents ship OPT-IN (--subagents / $DSC_SUBAGENTS=1).
The default tool set stays the M1 four, so the default system prompt is
byte-identical to the M1-gated prefix (verified) — no cache impact and
no re-gate for existing tasks.

Why it loses, and the limit of the claim:
- Per-child fixed overhead (own system prefix + multi-turn loop +
  report) is ~8k tokens; per-child work on this task is ~3.5k. Overhead
  exceeds work, so fan-out cannot win here. That is structural, not a
  tuning failure.
- The claim is therefore narrow: fan-out loses on SHALLOW independent
  lookups. Deep independent subtasks (per-child work >> per-child
  overhead) are UNTESTED. Do not read this as "sub-agents are useless";
  read it as "this task shape cannot pay for them".
- The solo arm never compacted (0/4) even on the 77 KB fixture: a
  competent agent greps rather than reading whole services, so fixture
  bytes do not become parent context pressure. The fan-out arm DID
  compact (parent carrying six reports). Bulk inflates the delegating
  arm, not the solo one — the opposite of the intuition that motivated
  bulking.

Caveats:
- Two earlier regimes, same direction: (a) small 11.5 KB fixture,
  uncapped — solo 17-19s/~24k tokens vs forced 6-way fan-out
  39s/~133k tokens; (b) small fixture at 24k budget — both arms passed
  with ZERO compactions and dsc spawned nothing, which is why the
  fixture was rebuilt to a realistic size.
- flash does NOT delegate spontaneously: with the task tool present but
  no instruction, it went solo every time, and a directive prompt
  guideline did not flip it. At this task size that choice is correct.
  Delegation POLICY is a separate open question from the mechanism.
- Rows tagged adapter "dsc-solo" were produced by the then-default-on
  build with sub-agents suppressed; comparable to today's plain `dsc`.
- The two 360s TIMEOUT rows (07:03, 07:09) predate the compaction
  task-pin fix and measure that bug, not either arm.

## parallel-fix — pinned 2026-08-05 (M4 deep-subtask gate: wall-clock MET, cost premium)

Four independent services (cart / schedule / parser / ledger), 8 real
contract bugs, each service with its own fail-fast suite; the top runner
reports all four at once so the task is not sequential by construction.
DSC_CONTEXT_BUDGET=60000. Fixture validated three ways before use:
unfixed fails, reference fixes pass, and gutting a test to force a pass
still fails (verify.sh hashes every test file).

This task exists because parallel-explore only measured SHALLOW lookups.
Per-child fixed overhead (~8k tokens) needed a task where per-child work
clearly exceeds it. Three prompt regimes were measured, and the spread
between them is the actual finding.

### A. Delegate-first prompt (canonical gate), n=3/arm

| adapter | success | wall s (med) | $/solve (med) | children | spawned on turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dsc (solo) | 3/3 | 66.8 | 0.0043 | - | - |
| dsc-fanout | 3/3 | 51.2 | 0.0072 | 4 | 2 |

Fan-out is 1.30x FASTER (0.77x wall) at 1.67x cost. Gate wording was
"beats single-agent wall-clock at comparable cost": wall-clock is MET;
"comparable cost" at +67% is a stretch, so the honest call is a
wall-clock win with a documented cost premium. In absolute terms the
premium is $0.0029/solve.

### B. "Use sub-agents" but WITHOUT "first", n=3/arm

| adapter | success | wall s (med) | $/solve (med) | children | spawned on turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dsc (solo) | 3/3 | 112.3 | 0.0051 | - | - |
| dsc-fanout | 3/3 | 233.2 | 0.0090 | 4 | ~10 |

Same mechanism, same fixture, instruction differing only in TIMING:
fan-out becomes 2.08x slower and 1.76x more expensive. The parent spent
9 turns reading all four services, then delegated fixes to children who
re-read everything. The context is paid for twice.

Delegation timing is the dominant variable, not parallelism. Verified
the mechanism is not at fault: transcripts show 4 spawns in ONE turn
followed by one wait, i.e. children genuinely run concurrently.

### C. Neutral prompt — no mention of sub-agents, n=3/arm

| adapter | success | wall s (med) | $/solve (med) | children |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dsc (solo) | 3/3 | 62.4 | 0.0041 | - |
| dsc-fanout (tool available) | 3/3 | 77.7 | 0.0047 | 0 |

With the task tool present AND a guideline explicitly telling it to
delegate before investigating, flash spawned NOTHING in 3/3 runs. It
does not delegate spontaneously — not with a permissive prompt
(parallel-explore), not with a directive guideline (here). Cost is
within noise of solo, so the tool's prefix weight is negligible; the
wall gap here is n=3 variance, not a real effect.

Consequences:
- Sub-agents stay OPT-IN. Not because default-on ships losing behavior
  (C shows it would be inert), but because it buys nothing flash will
  use on its own — the value is available to a CALLER who knows their
  task decomposes, not as an autonomous behavior.
- The M4 verdict is conditional, and both conditions are required:
  fan-out pays only when subtasks are DEEP and delegation happens
  BEFORE the parent absorbs the context. Miss either and it loses.
- parallel-explore's 6.75x cost blowout was the shallow END of this
  same curve, not a separate phenomenon.
- Caveat: n=3 per cell. The A-vs-B direction is large and consistent
  across every run, but treat the exact ratios as indicative.
